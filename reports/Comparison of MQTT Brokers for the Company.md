# Choosing an enterprise MQTT broker based on real readiness

**For a typical internal integration at a company, EMQX and HiveMQ Platform form the main production shortlist, while XMQ is worth considering as a conditional candidate for a narrower perimeter with in-house engineering work.** EMQX offers the broadest built-in set of connectors, processing rules and authentication options, but a production cluster of the current version requires a commercial license. HiveMQ Platform is strong in commercial extensions, Kafka and data quality control through Data Hub, but a significant part of these capabilities does not apply to the open Community Edition. XMQ implements core MQTT, clustering and webhook under MPL-2.0, but the reviewed tree lacks WebSocket, ready-made LDAP/OIDC/JWT integrations, Kafka/DB connectors, Prometheus/OpenTelemetry and Kubernetes tooling. The presence of a cluster in none of the products by itself proves message durability: regular EMQX sessions without Durable Sessions are kept in memory, HiveMQ depends on the number of replicas, and XMQ depends on HA of shared Redis and SQL. Therefore the decision must rest on failure scenarios, identity and data requirements, operational maturity and total cost of ownership, rather than on a latency comparison. Public load-test results from vendors, including those published by the XMQ project itself, are not treated in this report as independent evidence of superiority.

**Review date: November 5, 2026.** The study is based on official documentation and primary sources; independent load, conformance, chaos and security tests were not performed. For EMQX, the 6.3.x branch and [release 6.3.2 LTS](https://github.com/emqx/emqx/releases/tag/6.3.2) were reviewed; for HiveMQ — the [`latest` documentation](https://docs.hivemq.com/hivemq/latest/upgrade/index.html), [LTS 4.55](https://www.hivemq.com/changelog/whats-new-in-hivemq-4-55/) and [CE 2026.5](https://www.hivemq.com/changelog/hivemq-ce-2026-5-released); for XMQ — the tree at commit `36e0f16a49070af3a731ba564633ad96ec27f980`. The XMQ tree states version 1.1.1, but the changelog marks it as **unreleased**, so this information cannot be automatically transferred to a stable release; for a pilot a specific tag or commit must be pinned ([XMQ CHANGELOG](https://github.com/alexparshin1/xmq_public/blob/36e0f16a49070af3a731ba564633ad96ec27f980/CHANGELOG.md#L11-L17); locally: `CHANGELOG.md:11–17`, `VERSION.txt`).

These boundaries are fundamental for an honest comparison. The old EMQX before 5.9 under Apache 2.0 cannot be mixed with the current product: starting with 5.9, features are combined into a source-available BSL 1.1 package, and the free Additional Use Grant permits production only for independent single-node instances; a cluster of more than one node requires a Commercial License or Cloud ([EMQX Licensing FAQ](https://www.emqx.com/en/content/license-faq)). HiveMQ CE 2026.5 remains a current Apache-2.0 project, but the commercial Enterprise Extensions and the cluster and management functionality of the Platform do not thereby become CE capabilities ([HiveMQ CE](https://github.com/hivemq/hivemq-community-edition), [Enterprise Extensions](https://docs.hivemq.com/hivemq/latest/enterprise-extensions/index.html)). XMQ uses MPL-2.0 with file-level copyleft; individual extensions may have other terms, so license review must include them as well ([XMQ README](https://github.com/alexparshin1/xmq_public/blob/36e0f16a49070af3a731ba564633ad96ec27f980/README.md#L154-L174); locally: `README.md:154–174`, `LICENSE`).

## Protocols and HA require testing semantics, not feature names

### Basic MQTT is comparable, but transports and bridges differ

All three products claim MQTT 3.1/3.1.1/5.0, QoS 0–2, retained messages and shared subscriptions. For enterprise applications with native MQTT clients this is sufficient as a starting point, but **the browser scenario immediately narrows the choice**: EMQX and HiveMQ document WS/WSS, whereas XMQ's listeners schema allows only `MQTT` and `MQTT+SSL`, and the code creates either a TCP or an SSL listener ([EMQX WebSocket](https://docs.emqx.com/en/emqx/latest/develop/connect-emqx/mqtt-over-websocket.html), [HiveMQ Listeners](https://docs.hivemq.com/hivemq/latest/user-guide/listeners.html), [XMQ WSDL](https://github.com/alexparshin1/xmq_public/blob/36e0f16a49070af3a731ba564633ad96ec27f980/xmq.wsdl#L319-L356), [XMQ Server.cpp](https://github.com/alexparshin1/xmq_public/blob/36e0f16a49070af3a731ba564633ad96ec27f980/server/Server.cpp#L642-L679); locally: `xmq.wsdl:319–356`, `server/Server.cpp:642–679`). XMQ's Web UI on the HTTP port is not MQTT-over-WebSocket; browsers will require an external gateway.

EMQX additionally offers MQTT over QUIC, but the vendor explicitly calls it a non-standard MQTT variant: session state is not preserved after reconnection, subscriptions must be restored, and on unexpected closure of the data stream the QoS 1/2 state is lost. It cannot be considered a transparent replacement for a TCP persistent session ([EMQX QUIC](https://docs.emqx.com/en/emqx/latest/develop/mqtt-over-quic/introduction.html)). For none of the three products was an independent full MQTT 5 conformance test performed within the scope of this study; a protocol version claim does not replace verification of specific properties, enhanced authentication and edge cases of the SDKs used.

Bridges show especially clearly the difference between QoS support inside the broker and end-to-end delivery semantics. EMQX supports bidirectional Sink/Source, topic mappings and SQL transformations, but the bridge works only over MQTT over TCP/TLS, and the Source selects QoS 0 or 1 ([EMQX MQTT Bridge](https://docs.emqx.com/en/emqx/latest/develop/data-integration/data-bridge-mqtt.html)). The HiveMQ Enterprise Bridge is bidirectional and supports mapping and TLS/mTLS, however **all QoS 2 messages passing through it are downgraded to QoS 1** ([HiveMQ Bridge Extension](https://docs.hivemq.com/hivemq-bridge-extension/latest/index.html)). XMQ supports directions and per-topic QoS, but does not rewrite topic names and does not forward a message received through one bridge into the next: A–B–C transit and hub-and-spoke with delivery between spokes without direct links do not work ([XMQ bridge guide](https://github.com/alexparshin1/xmq_public/blob/36e0f16a49070af3a731ba564633ad96ec27f980/gui-react/src/documentation/SetupDocumentation.jsx#L605-L679); locally: `gui-react/src/documentation/SetupDocumentation.jsx:281–351,605–679`). Consequently, neither a "bidirectional bridge" nor MQTT QoS 2 proves end-to-end exactly-once between sites.

### Cluster availability and durability are designed separately

In EMQX, Core nodes of Mria store full data replicas, while Replicants receive updates asynchronously; a network with latency below 10 ms is recommended for Core. Regular persistent MQTT sessions by default remain in the node's memory and are lost when it restarts. **Durable Sessions**, introduced in 5.7 and disabled by default, store sessions and messages on local disk via RocksDB/Raft with configurable replication; NFS/SMB are not supported for embedded Durable Storage, and the price of durability is an additional disk and latency ([EMQX Mria](https://docs.emqx.com/en/emqx/latest/develop/cluster/mria-introduction.html), [EMQX Durable Sessions](https://docs.emqx.com/en/emqx/latest/guides/durability/durability_introduction.html)). For multiple sites EMQX offers separate clusters and asynchronous Cluster Linking, rather than a stretched WAN Core cluster ([Cluster Linking](https://docs.emqx.com/en/emqx/latest/develop/cluster-linking/introduction.html)). Within v6 rolling upgrades are documented, but migration v5→v6 loses Durable Sessions state — this is a mandatory rehearsal scenario for upgrades ([EMQX Rolling Upgrade](https://docs.emqx.com/en/emqx/latest/get-started/deploy/rolling-upgrades.html)).

HiveMQ Platform stores persistent data locally and replicates it between nodes. This set includes retained messages, subscriptions, persistent session queues and in-flight transfer state. By default `replica-count=2`, that is, there is an original and one copy; if more than that number of specific replica owners are lost, part of the persistent data may become unavailable. Therefore the failure of two arbitrary nodes cannot be declared safe based only on cluster size ([HiveMQ Cluster](https://docs.hivemq.com/hivemq/latest/user-guide/cluster.html)). The Platform supports per-node rolling upgrades within the published compatibility policy and backup/restore through Control Center/REST API ([HiveMQ Upgrade Policy](https://docs.hivemq.com/hivemq/latest/upgrade/index.html), [Backup & Restore](https://docs.hivemq.com/hivemq/4.55/user-guide/backup-restore.html)). For multi-site, the official scenario is regional clusters connected by Bridge; there is no current confirmation in the reviewed sources of a single WAN active-active cluster with strict RPO/RTO.

In XMQ, the coordinator and membership use Redis, and shared sessions/accounts/retained state rely on Redis and PostgreSQL/MySQL. Session takeover and cluster-wide shared subscriptions are implemented, but **loss of Redis for longer than the lease stops client service for the entire cluster**, and unavailability of SQL at startup prevents a node from starting ([XMQ Cluster Guide](https://github.com/alexparshin1/xmq_public/blob/36e0f16a49070af3a731ba564633ad96ec27f980/doc/Cluster.md#L229-L245), [SQL requirements](https://github.com/alexparshin1/xmq_public/blob/36e0f16a49070af3a731ba564633ad96ec27f980/doc/Cluster.md#L432-L465); locally: `doc/Cluster.md:24–81,193–245,312–381,432–465`). Consequently, HA configurations of Redis and SQL become part of the critical perimeter, not auxiliary services.

XMQ persistence is disabled by default; in standalone mode, if Redis is unavailable at startup, it continues to operate in memory, which can create an available but non-durable service. In addition, the documentation records loss windows due to write-behind, `max_queued_writes` and the chosen Redis fsync, loss of unacknowledged QoS 1 shared deliveries on node crash, and the possibility of losing an already acknowledged retained update before the Redis commit ([XMQ persistence](https://xmq.sptk.net/xmq_persistence), [XMQ cluster caveats](https://github.com/alexparshin1/xmq_public/blob/36e0f16a49070af3a731ba564633ad96ec27f980/doc/Cluster.md#L403-L418); locally: `gui-react/src/documentation/SetupDocumentation.jsx:252–266`, `doc/Cluster.md:72–81,403–418`, `CHANGELOG.md:446–456`). This does not make XMQ unsuitable automatically, but it requires agreeing on a measurable RPO: if zero loss of acknowledged messages is required, the current model cannot be accepted without successful proof in failure tests or an architecture change. No public rolling upgrade procedure with guaranteed mixed-version interoperability was found in the reviewed tree.

## Security, data and operations determine the scope of work

### Ready-made identity integrations differentiate the products

EMQX provides built-in authentication and authorization chains: LDAP, JWT/JWKS, external HTTP, MySQL/PostgreSQL/MongoDB/Redis, X.509/mTLS and other mechanisms; ACLs check publish/subscribe with allow/deny based on client attributes and topics ([EMQX Authentication](https://docs.emqx.com/en/emqx/latest/guides/access-control/authn/authn.html), [Authorization](https://docs.emqx.com/en/emqx/latest/guides/access-control/authz/authz.html)). OIDC/SAML SSO in the documentation found relates primarily to the Dashboard, while JWT/JWKS is confirmed for MQTT, so operator SSO cannot be passed off as a device OIDC flow. Namespaces provide quotas and configuration isolation, but topic/Client ID isolation is enabled separately; the administrative namespace is not designed for untrusted external tenants ([EMQX Namespace](https://docs.emqx.com/en/emqx/latest/guides/multi-tenancy/namespace-overview.html)). Initial configuration requires attention: MQTT authentication by default allows everyone, and the standard file authorizer contains a final allow-all; production must explicitly move to default-deny.

HiveMQ Platform implements enterprise security through extensions. ESE supports configurable pipelines and LDAP, SQL, JWT/OAuth, OIDC, X.509 realms with varying applicability to MQTT, Control Center and REST; the Java Extension SDK allows creating custom authenticators/authorizers ([HiveMQ ESE](https://docs.hivemq.com/hivemq-enterprise-security-extension/latest/ese.html), [Extension SDK](https://docs.hivemq.com/hivemq/latest/extensions/index.html)). The specific AD schema, group mapping and cache behavior when the directory is unavailable must be verified on the target stack: confirmed LDAP support does not prove compatibility with every Active Directory configuration. The test `hivemq-allow-all-extension` allows connections by default and must be removed; the authentication REST API also requires explicit configuration ([HiveMQ Installation](https://docs.hivemq.com/hivemq/latest/user-guide/install-hivemq.html), [REST API](https://docs.hivemq.com/hivemq/latest/rest-api/index.html)).

XMQ ships SQL accounts/groups, ordered allow/deny ACL for publish/subscribe and mTLS. This is sufficient for a closed perimeter with local accounts, but **LDAP/AD, OIDC/OAuth, MQTT-JWT and generic HTTP-auth are present only as directions for custom C/C++ extensions**, not as ready-made integrations ([XMQ user-database](https://github.com/alexparshin1/xmq_public/blob/36e0f16a49070af3a731ba564633ad96ec27f980/extension/user-database/UserDatabase.cpp#L15-L52), [Extension API](https://github.com/alexparshin1/xmq_public/blob/36e0f16a49070af3a731ba564633ad96ec27f980/extension/xmq_extension.h#L471-L563); locally: `extension/user-database/UserDatabase.cpp:15–52`, `extension/xmq_extension.h:471–563`, `extension/README.md:154–193`). The XMQ Web UI bearer token is issued after verification of local SQL credentials and does not mean federation with an external OIDC provider. In the reviewed API no documented CN→username mapping, CRL/OCSP, granular admin RBAC, tenant isolation or ready-made immutable audit log was found. These points are especially important if the certificate itself must determine MQTT identity and ACL subject.

### Built-in data pipelines reduce code but change delivery guarantees

EMQX has a SQL rule engine for filtering, enrichment and transformation, an extensive Sink catalog and a bidirectional Kafka connector. At the same time, external Sources in the reviewed documentation are limited to MQTT, Kafka and GCP Pub/Sub, so not every connector can be declared bidirectional ([EMQX Data Integration](https://docs.emqx.com/en/emqx/latest/develop/data-integration/data-bridges.html), [Kafka Integration](https://docs.emqx.com/en/emqx/latest/develop/data-integration/data-bridge-kafka.html)). Async request is enabled by default: an MQTT subscriber may receive a message before it is written to the external system, batching defers the write, and a full bounded buffer drops data. For a critical flow it is necessary to pin down sync/async, inflight, TTL, buffer capacity, fallback and deduplication on retry, rather than deriving exactly-once from the presence of QoS.

HiveMQ offers a commercial bidirectional Kafka Extension with mapping/transformers, multiple Kafka clusters and buffering when Kafka is unavailable; the extension must be installed on all nodes ([HiveMQ Kafka Extension](https://docs.hivemq.com/hivemq-kafka-extension/latest/index.html)). Data Hub adds JSON Schema/Protobuf validation, data/behavior policies, routing of invalid messages and JavaScript transformations ([HiveMQ Data Hub](https://docs.hivemq.com/hivemq/latest/data-hub/index.html)). This is a strong option when contract and data quality matter more than simple forwarding. The limitations are also significant: Data Hub Free is limited to one data policy **or** behavior policy, Modules are unavailable on Linux Arm64, and JavaScript represents integers exactly only up to `2^53−1`. No supported universal outbound HTTP/webhook connector was found in the reviewed catalog; the confirmed paths are a custom Java extension or Enterprise REST Service, whereas the management REST API itself does not replace a webhook.

XMQ ships an optional HTTP(S) webhook with topic filters, body template, custom headers, HMAC-SHA256 and retry/backoff. But its queue is bounded and in memory: **at-most-once semantics, without replay, durable queue or exactly-once**; under overload or restart records may be lost. The absence of a rule language, transformations, Kafka/DB/S3 connectors and per-tenant routing is explicitly documented ([XMQ Data Integration](https://github.com/alexparshin1/xmq_public/blob/36e0f16a49070af3a731ba564633ad96ec27f980/doc/DataIntegration.md#L19-L26); locally: `doc/DataIntegration.md:19–26`, `gui-react/src/documentation/DataIntegrationDocumentation.jsx:9–75`). The current distribution supports one endpoint per extension; the published web page with multiple `destinations` describes a removed prototype and diverges from the local changelog, configuration and documentation. For a decision, the pinned version of the sources should be used, not an outdated page ([XMQ CHANGELOG](https://github.com/alexparshin1/xmq_public/blob/36e0f16a49070af3a731ba564633ad96ec27f980/CHANGELOG.md#L38-L48); locally: `CHANGELOG.md:38–48`, `doc/DataIntegration.md:126–138`).

### XMQ observability will require a separate adapter

EMQX provides a Prometheus scrape API and export of OpenTelemetry metrics, traces and logs to an OTel Collector, as well as JSON/file logs, a Dashboard, an OpenAPI 3.0 management API and an audit log ([EMQX Prometheus](https://docs.emqx.com/en/emqx/latest/guides/observability/prometheus.html), [EMQX OpenTelemetry](https://docs.emqx.com/en/emqx/latest/observability/opentelemetry/opentelemetry.html), [EMQX REST API](https://docs.emqx.com/en/emqx/latest/guides/api.html)). External Collector, Grafana, storage and their HA are still deployed separately.

HiveMQ connects to Prometheus through an open-source monitoring extension, supports JMX/InfluxDB, structured JSON and Syslog; Control Center and the OpenAPI 3.0 REST API cover operational operations, backup and Data Hub ([HiveMQ Monitoring](https://docs.hivemq.com/hivemq/latest/user-guide/monitoring.html), [HiveMQ Logging](https://docs.hivemq.com/hivemq/latest/user-guide/logging.html)). OpenTelemetry in the reviewed source means a separate **commercial Distributed Tracing Extension for traces**, not a universal OTLP export of all metrics and logs ([HiveMQ Distributed Tracing](https://www.hivemq.com/products/extensions/distributed-tracing-extension)).

XMQ provides `$SYS`, `GetStatistics`, a JSON control API, a React Web UI and file/stdout logs. However, `$SYS` is node-local, historical metrics and alerts remain incomplete tasks, and no ready-made Prometheus exporter or OpenTelemetry integration was found in the production source/docs ([XMQ SystemStatistics](https://github.com/alexparshin1/xmq_public/blob/36e0f16a49070af3a731ba564633ad96ec27f980/server/SystemStatistics.cpp#L26-L48), [XMQ cluster tasks](https://github.com/alexparshin1/xmq_public/blob/36e0f16a49070af3a731ba564633ad96ec27f980/doc/Cluster.md#L619-L629); locally: `server/SystemStatistics.cpp:26–48`, `xmq.wsdl:1356–1445`, `doc/Cluster.md:9–14,619–629`). The company will need its own collector/adapter, dashboards, alert rules and cluster-wide aggregation; this work must be included in the cost estimate and not remain a hidden "free" addition.

## Deployment and licensing change the total cost of ownership

EMQX is deployed from Linux packages, Docker and Kubernetes; Operator 3.0 requires Kubernetes 1.27+ and supports current 5.9/5.10/6.x ([EMQX Installation](https://docs.emqx.com/en/emqx/latest/get-started/deploy/install.html), [EMQX Operator](https://docs.emqx.com/en/emqx/latest/get-started/deploy/kubernetes/operator/operator.html)). For an HA pilot a trial or commercial license is required: the free single-node is functionally useful but does not test a production topology. The trial as of the review date is limited to 15 days and does not permit production; commercial licenses may include session/TPS limits ([EMQX License](https://docs.emqx.com/en/emqx/latest/get-started/deploy/license.html)). The cost of self-managed Enterprise and BYOC must be requested for specific nodes, sessions/TPS, support and sites; no price is assumed in the report.

HiveMQ Platform targets production Linux, Docker and Kubernetes with a current Platform Operator; the current local setup requires Java 21 or 25 ([HiveMQ Getting Started](https://docs.hivemq.com/hivemq/latest/user-guide/getting-started.html), [Platform Operator](https://docs.hivemq.com/hivemq-platform-operator/introduction.html)). Public self-managed Lab/Launch/Run plans are limited to one broker and their own connection/message-rate limits, while the clustered multi-broker/multi-site scenario belongs to Enterprise with individual terms ([HiveMQ Self-managed Pricing](https://www.hivemq.com/pricing)). At the same time, the pricing page lists "All Enterprise Extensions" in the plans, while the extension documentation speaks of separate licensing. **The sources diverge**, so before the pilot a written entitlements matrix for the specific order form must be obtained, including ESE, Bridge, Kafka, Data Hub and tracing ([Enterprise Extension Licensing](https://docs.hivemq.com/hivemq/latest/enterprise-extensions/index.html)).

XMQ provides packages for the Linux/FreeBSD/Windows listed in the guide, systemd/rc.d/service commands and an amd64 Docker image. No official Helm chart, Kubernetes operator/manifests, arm64 packages or production reference deployment were found in the reviewed tree ([XMQ Install Guide](https://github.com/alexparshin1/xmq_public/blob/36e0f16a49070af3a731ba564633ad96ec27f980/gui-react/src/documentation/InstallDocumentation.jsx#L100-L161), [XMQ Docker](https://github.com/alexparshin1/xmq_public/blob/36e0f16a49070af3a731ba564633ad96ec27f980/docker/README.md#L1-L15); locally: `gui-react/src/documentation/InstallDocumentation.jsx:7–59,100–161`, `docker/README.md:1–15,46–55,74–108`). Kubernetes will require its own wiring for configuration, certificates, service discovery, Redis/SQL HA and an upgrade runbook. The hard dependency on a compatible SPTK and the extension ABI change from 1.2 to 1.3 mean that the entire set of binaries and extensions must be updated and tested.

The XMQ project is young: the public repository was created on September 27, 2026, and the GitHub API as of the review date showed one visible contributor; this is an indicator of ecosystem and bus-factor risk, but not a standalone assessment of code quality ([XMQ Repository API](https://api.github.com/repos/alexparshin1/xmq_public), [Contributors API](https://api.github.com/repos/alexparshin1/xmq_public/contributors?per_page=100)). The documentation also contains contradictions: the README calls the cluster incomplete, while the guide, changelog and code confirm its presence; the old limit of 10 nodes diverges from the current `Coordinator::MaxMembers = 50`; the extension README states ABI 1.2 versus 1.3 in the header. For the contract and pilot, the version must be pinned and a current supported feature matrix required ([XMQ Cluster Docs](https://github.com/alexparshin1/xmq_public/blob/36e0f16a49070af3a731ba564633ad96ec27f980/gui-react/src/documentation/ClusterDocumentation.jsx#L8-L42), [Coordinator.h](https://github.com/alexparshin1/xmq_public/blob/36e0f16a49070af3a731ba564633ad96ec27f980/server/Cluster/Coordinator.h#L58-L62); locally: `README.md:131–135`, `gui-react/src/documentation/ClusterDocumentation.jsx:8–42`, `server/Cluster/Coordinator.h:58–62`, `extension/xmq_extension.h:94–96`, `extension/README.md:205–213`). The site offers free support without a speed guarantee and commercial e-mail support from Linotex with published reaction/fix SLA options; applicability to production, incident boundaries and cost must be confirmed by contract ([XMQ Support](https://xmq.sptk.net/support)).

**Cloud cannot be equated with self-hosted.** For EMQX, the Serverless, Dedicated and BYOC plans differ in durability, message limits and number of connectors; for HiveMQ Cloud, Starter/Enterprise differ in availability of Data Hub, Bridge, tracing and Prometheus endpoint ([EMQX Plans](https://www.emqx.com/en/pricing), [HiveMQ Cloud Plans](https://www.hivemq.com/pricing/fully-managed)). Creation of HiveMQ Cloud Serverless is already unavailable, and existing Serverless clusters will stop accepting MQTT on December 31, 2026; this does not apply to Starter/Enterprise ([HiveMQ Cloud Notice](https://docs.hivemq.com/hivemq-cloud/quick-start-guide.html)). For XMQ no managed cloud was found in the reviewed sources. Requirements for data residency, air-gapped support, regions and the actual SLA must be verified separately; independent security or compliance certifications were not confirmed in the study and are not claimed here.

## Scenario-based selection is completed only by an acceptance pilot

### Recommendations for typical scenarios

**For heterogeneous enterprise integration — MQTT→Kafka/DB/cloud, filtering and transformation without a separate ETL — EMQX should be the first candidate.** Its advantage lies in the breadth of its built-in rule engine, Sink catalog and identity backends. The selection condition is a commercial license for HA and successful tests of Durable Sessions, buffer overflow and retry/dedup of the selected connectors. If what is needed above all is ready-made Kafka streams, strict contract validation and data quality policies, **HiveMQ Platform becomes an equal or stronger candidate**, especially if the team is ready to maintain Java extensions. The decision between them should be made on the same real set of integrations, not on the total number of logos in the catalog.

**For browser clients and WSS, choose EMQX or HiveMQ**, if you are not ready to add and maintain an external WebSocket gateway in front of XMQ. **For centralized LDAP/AD/JWT/OIDC**, EMQX and HiveMQ require less in-house development; XMQ should be evaluated together with the cost, security review and lifecycle of a new extension. **For multi-site**, independent regional clusters with explicitly designed flows via EMQX Cluster Linking or HiveMQ Bridge are preferable; XMQ is suitable only for topologies where direct pairwise links are sufficient and transit or topic rewrite is not needed. In all cases, loops, duplicates, retained messages and the actual QoS on the inter-broker path should be tested separately.

**It is rational to pilot XMQ for an internal TCP/TLS perimeter with local SQL accounts/ACL, a limited number of direct bridges and a best-effort webhook**, when MPL-2.0 and source code control are important and the team is ready to build observability, Kubernetes/deployment automation and the missing identity/data integrations. It also allows exploring clustering without the EMQX/HiveMQ Platform license barrier, but this does not eliminate the costs of Redis/SQL HA and operational engineering. If requirements include durable transactional outbox, a ready-made Prometheus/OTel stack, WSS, strict certificate identity lifecycle or a guaranteed rolling upgrade "out of the box", XMQ in the current reviewed tree does not cover them without external components. For a small production perimeter where HA is not required, a free single-node EMQX, HiveMQ CE/Launch and XMQ are all functionally possible, but they are different licenses and feature sets; such a pilot cannot be presented as validation of a clustered solution.

### Specific pilot acceptance checklist

The pilot must run on one target infrastructure, with the same client scenarios, message sizes, TLS/auth, persistence and enabled integrations. Before the start, the architecture team approves numerical SLOs, RPO/RTO, the acceptable number of duplicates and the reconnect budget; the absence of a pre-set threshold makes the result unsuitable for an objective choice.

| № | Check and procedure | Mandatory acceptance criterion / evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pin the configuration:** version/commit, OS, JDK/SPTK, extensions and ABI, number of nodes, replica/DS settings, Redis/SQL/Kafka versions, license and limits | Reproducible BOM and configurations in the repository; written entitlements and the right to run this cluster pilot; for XMQ, use of `unreleased` is excluded without an explicit decision to pin the commit |
| 2 | **Test the protocol with real SDKs:** MQTT 3.1.1/5.0, QoS 0/1/2, retained, LWT, shared subscriptions, session expiry, reconnect, all required MQTT 5 properties; separately WS/WSS if a browser is needed | All mandatory scenarios pass automated tests; any incompatibilities found are entered in a blocking list; the absence of WSS in XMQ is not masked by testing TCP only |
| 3 | **Test security positively and negatively:** valid/invalid/expired credentials, default-deny, publish/subscribe ACL, revocation of rights of an active client, mTLS, revoked certificate, unavailability of LDAP/IdP/SQL/HTTP auth | No allow-all on listeners and the management plane; unauthorized operations are rejected and logged; behavior during auth outage matches the approved fail-open/fail-close; XMQ certificate identity and revocation are either proven or the requirement is closed by an external solution |
| 4 | **Perform fault injection under load:** kill/restart broker, loss of node/AZ, network partition, topology change; for XMQ, separately disable Redis longer than the lease and SQL at startup/runtime | Availability and recovery fit within the approved RTO; no split-brain and no unexplained state loss; XMQ HA Redis/SQL is tested as part of the product, and standalone in-memory fallback is detected by an alert |
| 5 | **Measure durability with a log of unique IDs:** acknowledged QoS 1/2, offline queue, retained updates, restart/crash at the moment of write and delivery | Losses and duplicates are compared with the pre-approved RPO; for zero RPO any loss of an acknowledged message blocks release; the documented XMQ shared QoS 1 and retained-before-Redis-commit windows are explained separately |
| 6 | **Test federation:** bidirectional flows, overlapping filters, reconnect, loop prevention, retained/QoS mapping, A–B–C transit if required | No loop storm; actual QoS and topic mapping match the design; HiveMQ QoS2→1 is accepted by the architecture or the scenario is rejected; XMQ without transit/rewrite is not used in an incompatible topology |
| 7 | **Break the integrations:** stop Kafka/DB/HTTP endpoint, return 4xx/5xx/429/timeout, overflow the buffer, expire TTL, restart the broker during backlog | The approved loss/duplicate semantics are observed; drop/overflow/TTL counters and alerts exist; replay/recovery is tested where required; XMQ at-most-once webhook is not accepted for a durable or exactly-once flow |
| 8 | **Rehearse upgrade and rollback:** from the source production version to the target, with clients and queues, including extensions and the DB schema; separately verify prohibitions on config changes during the process | Connections, sessions and messages behave within SLO/RPO; rollback restores service; EMQX v5→v6 DS loss is taken into account; XMQ mixed-version is not allowed without proven compatibility and a dedicated runbook |
| 9 | **Test observability and management:** dashboards by node and cluster, Prometheus/OTel or adapter, centralized logs, alert rules, backup/restore, audit and operator RBAC | The on-call engineer detects failure and backlog without manually logging into each node; restore from backup is tested on a separate perimeter; for XMQ, cluster-wide aggregation, historical storage, alerts and recording of administrative actions work |
| 10 | **Conduct your own load test:** steady load, burst, reconnect storm, large number of topics/ACL/subscriptions, persistence and connectors enabled; record throughput, p95/p99 latency, CPU/RAM/disk/network and errors | All approved SLOs are met with capacity headroom for the failure of one node; results and harness are published within the company; vendor self-benchmarks are not used as a substitute for this run |
| 11 | **Assess operations and TCO:** cost of licenses/support, Redis/SQL/SSD/LB/Kafka/monitoring infrastructure, multi-site traffic, backups, development and maintenance of adapters/extensions, training of on-call staff | There is a comparable estimate for at least the operating horizon chosen by the company; owners of each component and runbooks are assigned; support, SLA and upgrade policy are confirmed in writing |
| 12 | **Conduct a final architecture/security review** based on the failure log, open limitations and deviations from requirements | All critical rows 1–11 are closed with evidence; exceptions have an owner, a deadline and a compensating control; otherwise the decision is not moved to production |

## Conclusion

The choice is not about which broker is "faster", but about how much critical functionality the company gets as a supported product and how much it takes on as its own platform development. EMQX and HiveMQ require careful comparison of commercial rights, but cover a noticeably larger part of the identity, data and operations perimeter; XMQ opens the source code and a basic clustering model under MPL-2.0, but shifts a significant part of integration and operational responsibility to the team.

The main practical result of the study is the need to compare **specific versions, editions and failure scenarios**, not brands. If the pilot shows equal suitability of EMQX and HiveMQ, the decisive factors will be written entitlements, the cost of the required extensions and convenience for the existing team; if XMQ is chosen for openness, its adoption must include a separate budget and owners for HA dependencies, observability, security extensions and the update lifecycle.
